# Use and Trade Consistency
Ensure that the Use and Trade section follows the below specifications and is overall consistent in the information that is presented.

If the species is not utilised, it does not need a narrative or classification scheme. No feedback needed.

Uses outlined in the narrative should be consistent with those in the classifciation scheme.

## Section Content Requirements: Use and Trade [Red List Standards Consistency (Section 2.2.5)]
This section is no longer strictly required for assessments to be published on the IUCN Red List. However, this
is very useful information therefore it is recommended that this be recorded for those taxa that are
utilized, including taxa that are legally or illegally hunted or collected.
In SIS, this information is recorded in the General notes regarding trade and use of the species text
field. This text should summarize the information currently available for any utilization and/or trade
of the taxon (including legal and illegal hunting and collection, and for local, national and
international trade).

### Preferred Style
Although this species is legally protected in all of
its range countries, illegal collection continues in
many parts of its range. Hunters particularly target
young individuals for the international pet trade.

### Try to Avoid
Illegal collection.

## Use and Trade Classification Scheme [Red List Standards Consistency (Section 2.4.6)]
Ensure that all relevant uses (those given in the narrative) have been listed in the use and trade classification scheme section if available.

Coding of the end use (purpose) and scale of utilization of the taxon is not required, but is
recommended supporting information for all taxa that are utilized.
The main purpose of recording use and trade information is to allow these data to be analysed: this
information is useful for informing international, regional and national trade regulations (e.g,.CITES).

In addition to the use and trade text field (section 2.2.5), SIS includes a Use and Trade Classification Scheme that allows you to code utilized taxa for:
- Purpose of any use (e.g., for food, medicine, pet trade, research, etc.).
- Source of harvested individuals (e.g., from wild or captive populations).
- Form removed (e.g., whole individuals or parts of individuals: eggs, seeds, fruit, etc.)
- Level of trade (subnational, national or international)
- Harvest level (in terms of volume, weight or number of individuals)
- Whether harvesting for this use is a possible threat to the species.

## Non-Consumptive Use Classification Scheme (Version 1.0)

This scheme records non-consumptive use of species in the wild, where individuals or parts are not harvested but are targets for use in other ways (e.g. tourism, education, research, cultural or spiritual purposes).

| Field Name | Options | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Non-consumptive use of the species? | Check box | Whether the species is used non-consumptively. |
| Explanation of non-consumptive use? | Text field | Free text description of how the species is used non-consumptively. |
